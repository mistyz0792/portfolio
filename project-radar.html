K-LD7RADAR NAV

A robot with no encoders and no IMU. One stationary radar tells it where it is.

scroll to watch the run

01

The radar goes blind
when it spins

The K-LD7 is a Doppler unit — it only measures radial velocity. A robot turning on the spot produces none of it, so it vanishes from the plot. The routine spins left until there is enough motion to lock on, giving up after 12 seconds.

02

Drive 30 cm first.
Then you know which way you face.

No compass, no IMU — so heading comes out of the trail the radar recorded. Drive 30 centimetres straight, then take the start-to-end vector. Less than 5 cm of travel is noise, and the run aborts.

03

Open-loop rotation,
because there is no alternative

The radar cannot measure a turn in place, so rotation is timed against a calibrated rate. That rate is never exactly right, so it always overshoots — and the next cycle is what corrects it, up to three times.

  • L / RROTATE
  • FFORWARD
  • SSTOP
  • QQUERY TRIM

04

Inside the
30 cm window

Seven degrees long on the turn, 192 centimetres of drive, and it still stops 25 centimetres from the goal — inside the arrival threshold. That is the accuracy a system with no rotation sensing can actually deliver.
